# back

<br>

Song: I'm still standing, Elton John

<br>

Two and a half years since my last post. Much has happened, much has changed, much has improved!

<br>

I sit here thinking about the past two and a half years and find myself struggling to reflect. I have a hard time looking back on stretches of time, regardless of their significance or how recently they passed. I seem to be in a phase of life where time moves along without leaving clear markers in my memory. At first glance, I look back and don't see much change. But when I slow down and put some thought into it, there is more there than I initially give myself credit for. I suppose writing serves a good purpose after all: making room to pause, remember, and recognize how far life has moved along...

<br>

For one, I went from working a dead-end filler job to spending the past year in a position that offers a career path and puts my skills and talents to good use. Six and a half years after retiring from service, I finally found a role at a company that suits me well. Working remotely in an environment that encourages independence, initiative, and a flexible work-life balance has been immensely beneficial. I have found personal clarity, professional confidence, and a sense of belonging on a winning team. My effort has produced results that exceeded expectations and earned high praise, and I am grateful for the opportunity to contribute and feel good about the work I do. It feels good to look ahead with confidence and curiosity about where this part of the journey may lead.

<br>

The absolute greatest blessing of my life is being a father and watching these three girls grow over the years. This is also where the passing of time feels the most real. I can struggle to see what has changed in my own life, then look at them and wonder how so much growing happened so quickly. Each is becoming her own person, with her own thoughts, interests, and way of seeing the world. There is so much to look forward to as they grow, even while I wish some of these years would slow down a little. Being their dad gives me a reason to keep working on myself and to think carefully about the example I set. I want to be someone they can count on, someone who listens, and someone they never have to question loves them. I won't get every part of fatherhood right, but I want to keep showing up and doing my best. When I think about what I hope to remember from these years, it is the time spent with them, including the ordinary days that are easy to overlook while living them. I am grateful I get to be their dad.

<br>

Reflect on the past to guide a better future, but give the positives at least as much attention as the negatives. There is a lot here worth remembering, and a lot ahead worth looking forward to. It helps to write it down.
